Kapital ubija?

GENTRIFICATION?

The Dictionary of Standard Slovene defines the term gentrification as:

“the transformation of predominantly residential urban districts with a local population, through new construction and renovation, into districts with tourist and higher-end service uses.” [1] The word was coined and first used by the British sociologist Ruth Glass while observing the movement of the middle, wealthier class (“the gentry”) into poorer working-class districts of London. The definition found in the dictionary is in line with Glass’s observations:

“One by one, many of the working-class quarters of London have been invaded by the middle classes – upper and lower. Shabby, modest mews and cottages – two rooms up and two down – have been taken over, when their leases have expired, and have become elegant, expensive residences. (...) Once this process of ‘gentrification’ starts in a district, it goes on rapidly until all or most of the original working-class occupiers are displaced, and the whole social character of the district is changed.”

[2, Glass, 1964, xviii – xix]

Sharon Zukin, professor emerita of sociology, analysed the transformation of SoHo in New York. Artists began moving into this mostly working-class part of the city, setting up studios and apartments in older, affordable buildings. [3] The artists brought “cultural capital” [4] to the neighbourhood, which attracted higher social classes who, over time and with greater purchasing power, pushed the artists out. According to Bourdieu, cultural capital refers to non-economic resources such as knowledge, skills, familiarity with norms and values, and cultural goods. [5, 1986]

Gary Bridge, a social and urban geographer, notes that the distribution and circulation of cultural capital are key forces shaping the fate of cities in metropolitan areas around the world, particularly in the context of the changes brought about by gentrification. [6, in Velkavrh, 26]

Neil Smith, a geographical theorist, stresses that gentrification is part of a wider process of urban development. He sees the problem not in the demand of a new middle class but in the supply side of the property market. He argues that the key driver of gentrification is the growing gap between the potential ground rent and the capitalised value of land. This rent gap is exploited by estate agents and developers, who first deliberately devalue a neighbourhood and then renovate it for profit. [7, Smith in Hekič]

Hackworth explains gentrification as “the production of space for progressively more affluent users” [6, in Velkavrh, 27]. We can conclude that the key difference between the gentrification of an area and its renovation or revitalisation is that gentrification always leads to the displacement or disintegration of the original community. Because of the raised standard, the original residents are forced to leave the gentrified area.

GENTRIFICATION IN LJUBLJANA?

The critical geographer David Harvey, an advocate of Lefebvre’s concept of “the right to the city”, describes the change in how cities are governed under neoliberalism as a shift from a managerial to an entrepreneurial culture. The old city was organised around the factory; the new city, run like a company, is organised around consumption. [8] Today, cities are increasingly concerned with how to attract as much capital and as many consumers as possible. Zukin observes: “The concept has shifted from the quality of people to the quality of things and, more recently, to the quality of experience.” [9]

The city dweller becomes at the same time a tourist who experiences the city and, above all, spends money, while quality of life is sold as a tourist attraction. [8] Ploštajner points out that the renovation of Ljubljana’s city centre follows this entrepreneurial strategy: “Only a decade ago we could be outraged that the city centre was decaying because consumption had moved to BTC; now we are witnessing the revival of the centre, which has, however, become nothing more than a shopping centre, carefully arranged according to the principles of ‘new urbanism’.” [8]

Something similar is happening to public spaces: they are becoming spaces of spectacle.

“In societies where modern conditions of production prevail, all of life presents itself as an immense accumulation of spectacles.” [10, Debord] Debord describes the spectacle as “a social relationship between people that is mediated by images.” [11]

Ploštajner describes how a space of spectacle comes into being: “Car parks are disappearing from the squares and concert stages are growing in their place; there are no longer cars in the streets but market stalls; the riverbanks are no longer empty but full of bars.” [8]

If cities were once a refuge for the most driven, creative, innovative and even strange individuals and communities, gentrification means exactly the opposite. It displaces precisely those individuals and communities, who can no longer afford the city. A place where new trends, cultural movements and innovation are born becomes a spectacle of culture, goods and services. Everyday urban life is forced to give way to a spectacle that turns the city into a machine for generating private profit. [12]

CAPITAL KILLS?

The renovation of the former Rog factory provoked a strong reaction. Before the renovation, the squatters were violently evicted from the factory and their equipment destroyed. An anonymous user, active since 2002 in the struggle of the Erased as a researcher, activist, supporter and companion, writes: “It was not only the Erased and the migrant community that were left without their political home; numerous collectives working in sport, circus, music, dance, creative writing, queer-anarchist self-organisation, theatre, community cooking and eating, and bonds of solidarity and friendship lost their spaces and their dreams. The skate park was destroyed, as were the circus hall, the concert hall, mountains of books, computer equipment and so on. As Irfan Beširović said, the destruction of spaces, equipment and archives, including banners, posters, assembly minutes and so on, is reminiscent of book burnings in totalitarian regimes.” [13]

The idea of the original group Temp, which occupied the factory in 2006, was to create a space for creativity, which the city lacked at the time. “Soon a new space for dialogue, cooperation, direct democracy and assembly was established – with the aim of creating a place of innovation and new ways of exploring.” [14, Grabar] The idea that had shaped the Rog Autonomous Factory before the renovation was realised once again, after the eviction and renovation, in the Rog Centre. The existing users were pushed out of the area, and the space became part of the entrepreneurial city organised around consumption. The authenticity of the Rog Autonomous Factory was replaced by a space of spectacle of culture, goods and services.

Architecturally, the project is a good example of the renovation of existing building fabric. The architecture is restrained yet of high quality. Boris Bežan, the architect who leads studio BAX, said in an interview: “We want the building to truly come alive. It is flexible enough to accommodate any kind of programme. Younger people in particular want spaces that allow individuals to take part democratically. I hope Rog will give them at least some of its spaces: that they will find their workshop there, where they can do everything they would normally do in their parents’ garage. I hope they will find in Rog what is missing from their everyday lives.” [15]

“Just as it was not possible to erase people, the idea of the Rog Autonomous Factory cannot be erased or destroyed either. The spaces have been destroyed; the ideas and we, the people, have survived. Although a large part of the archive of the Erased was destroyed, we pass the history of this struggle – which is also a struggle against gentrification – on to the generations to come.” [13]

Gentrification is a major challenge for the future development of urban areas. As long as cities develop in the direction of consumption and spectacle, they can quickly succumb to gentrification and become spaces for ever more affluent users. Any renovation or revitalisation of the urban fabric must be carefully considered, so that it builds the authenticity of the city rather than destroying the existing community. While the renovation of the Rog factory is a high-quality example of renovation, the investor’s cooperation with the existing users represents the very opposite. Instead of participatory design, there was violent eviction and displacement. Does capital kill? In the case of Rog, the answer is yes. Capital that kills for some builds for others. Rather than asking whether capital kills, we should ask whether capital truly contributes to building community.

GENTRIFIKACIJA?

Slovar slovenskega knjižnega jezika termin gentrifikacija opredeljuje kot:

»spreminjanje pretežno bivalnih mestnih predelov z lokalnim prebivalstvom s pozidavo, prenovo v predele s turistično, storitveno namembnostjo višjega razreda.« [1] Besedo je skovala ter prvič uporabila britanska sociologinja Ruth Glass, ko je v Londonu opazovala preseljevanje srednjega, premožnejšega sloja (»the gentry«) v revnejše predele delavskega razreda. Definicja besede, ki jo najdemo v SSKJ je v skladu z opažanji Glassove:

»Ena za drugo so mnoge četrti delavskega razreda v Londonu zasedli srednji razredi, zgornji in spodnji. Zanikrne, skromne hiše in koče z dvema prostoroma zgoraj in dvema spodaj so bile prevzete, ko so jim potekle najemne pogodbe, in so postale elegantna, draga bivališča. (...) Ko se ta proces 'gentrifikacije' začne v neki četrti, poteka hitro, dokler niso vsi ali večina prvotnih prebivalcev delavskega razreda izpodrinjeni, in se celoten socialni značaj četrti spremeni.«

[2, Glass, 1964, xviii – xix]

Sharon Zukin, profesorica emerita sociologije, je analizirala spreminjanje predela SoHo v New Yorku. V večinoma delavski predel mesta so se začeli priseljevati umetniki, ki so si v starejših, cenovno dostopnih hišah začeli urejati studie in stanovanja. [3] Umetniki so v sosesko prinesli »kulturni kapital«[4], kar je pritegnilo višje družbene razrede, ki so s časom zaradi večje kupne moči umetnike izrinili. Po Bordieu se kulturni kapital nanaša na neekonomske vire, kot so znanje, veščine, poznavanje norm in vrednot ter kulturne dobrine. [5, 1986]

Gary Bridge, socialni urbani geograf, omenja, da sta distirbucija in kroženje kulturnega kapitala ključni sili, ki vplivata na usodo mest v metropolitanskih območjih po svetu, predvsem v okviru sprememb, ki jih povzroča gentrifikacija.[6, v Velkavrh, 26]

Neil Smith, geografski teoretik, poudarja, da je gentrifikacija del širšega procesa urbanega razvoja. Ne vidi težav v povpraševanju novega srednjega razreda, temveč v ponudbi na nempremičninskem trgu. Pudarja, da je ključni dejavnik gentrifikacije vse večja razlika med potencialno vrednostjo zemljiščne rente in kapitalizirano vrednostjo zemljišča. Ustvarjeno rentno vrzel izkoriščajo nepremičninski agenti in razvijalci tako, da najprej načrtno razvrednotijo sosesko, nato pa jo za dobiček obnovijo. [7, Smith v Hekič]

Hackworth gentrifikacijo razloži kot »produkcijo prostora za postopoma vse bolj premožne uporabnike« [6, v Velkavrh, 27] Sklepamo lahko, da je ključna razlika med gentrifikacijo nekega območja in prenovo ali revitalizacijo v tem, da gentrifikacija zmeraj vodi do izrinjenja ali razpada prvotne skupnosti. Zaradi zvišanega standarda so prvotni prebivalci primorani zapustiti gentrificirano območje.

GENTRIFIKACIJA V LJUBLJANI?

Kritični geograf David Harvey, ki je zagovornik Lefebvrejevega koncepta »Pravica do mesta«, spremembo v načinu upravljanja mest, v okoliščinah neoliberalizma, opiše kot pomik od menedžerske kulture k podjetniški. Staro mesto je bilo organizirano okoli tovarne, novo mesto, ki je kot podjetje, pa je organizirano okoli porabe. [8] Mesta se danes vse bolj ukvarjajo s tem kako privabiti čim več kapitala ter porabnikov. Zukin ugotavlja: »Koncept se je preselil od kvalitete ljudi h kvaliteti stvari in nedavno h kvaliteti izkušnje.«[9]

Prebivalec mesta postaja hkrati turist, ki mesto doživlja in predvsem troši denar, kvaliteta življenja pa se prodaja kot turistična znamenitost.[8] Ploštajner poudarja, da se prenova Ljubljanjskega mestenga središča ravna po tej podjetniški strategiji: »Še pred desetletjem smo se lahko zgražali nad tem, da središče mesta propada, ker se je poraba preselila v BTC, zdaj pa smo priča oživljanju mestnega središča, ki pa je postalo zgolj premišljeno in v skladu z načeli »novega urbanizma« urejeno trgovsko središče.«[8]

Podobno se dogaja z javnimi prostori, postajajo prostori spektakla.

»V družbah, kjer vladajo moderne produkcijske razmere, se celotno življenje kaže kot neizmerno kopičenje spektaklov.« [10, Debord] Debord spektakel razlaga kot »medosebni družbeni odnos, katerega posrednik so podobe.«[11]

Ploštajner opisuje proces nastajanja prostora spektakla:»S trgov se umikajo parkirišča in na njih rastejo koncertni odri, na ulicah ni več avtomobilov, ampak stojnice, nabrežja niso več prazna, temveč polna lokalov.«[8]

Če so včasih mesta predstavljala zatočišče najbolj propulzivnih, ustvarjalnih, inovativnih, tudi čudnih posameznikov in skupnosti, gentrifikacija pomeni ravno obratno. Povzroči izrinjenje ravno teh posameznikov in skupnosti, mesta si več ne morejo privoščiti. Prostor rojevanja novih trendov, kulturnih gibanj in inovacije, postane spektakel kulture, dobrin in storitev. Vsakdanje meščansko življenje, se je primorano umakniti spektaklu, ki mesto spremeni v stroj za ustvarjanje zasebnih dobičkov. [12]

KAPITAL UBIJA?

Prenova nekdanje tovarne Rog je vzbudila bujen odziv. Skvoterji so bili pred prenovo iz tovarne nasilno izgnani, njihova oprema pa uničena. Anonimna uporabnica, ki je od leta 2002 aktivno dejavna v boju izbrisanih kot raziskovalka, aktivistka, podpornica in sopotnica, zapisuje: »Ob tem brez svojega političnega doma niso ostali le izbrisani in migrantska skupnost, ampak so brez prostorov in svojih sanj ostali številni kolektivi na področju športa, cirkusa, glasbe, plesa, kreativnega pisanja, kvir-anarhističnega samoorganiziranja, gledališča, skupnostnega kuhanja in prehranjevanja, solidarnostnih in prijateljskih vezi, uničen je bil tako skejt park kot cirkusarna, koncertna dvorana, gore knjig, računalniška oprema in tako dalje. Kot je dejal Irfan Beširović – uničevanje prostorov, opreme in arhiva, vključno s transparenti, plakati, zapisniki skupščin in tako dalje spominja na zažiganje knjig v totalitarnih režimih.« [13]

Ideja prvotne skupine Temp, ki je tovarno zasedla leta 2006, je bila vzpostavitev prostora ustvarjalnosti, ki ga je takrat v mestu primanjkovalo. »Kmalu se vzpostavi nov prostor za dialog, sodelovanje, neposredno demokracijo, skupščino – z namenom ustvariti mesto inovacij in novih načinov raziskovanja.« [14, Grabar] Ideja, ki je oblikovala Avtonomno tovarno Rog že pred prenovo, se je po evikciji in prenovi ponovno udejanjila v Centru Rog. Obstoječi uporabniki so bili z območja izrinjeni, prostor pa je postal del podjetniškega mesta, ki je organizirano okoli porabe. Avtentičnost Avtonomne tovarne Rog je nadomestil prostor spektakla kulture, dobrin in storitev.

Projekt z arhitekturnega vidika predstavlja dober primer prenove obstoječega stavbnega tkiva. Arhitektura je zadržana, a hkrati kvalitetna. Boris Bežan, arhitekt, ki vodi studio BAX, je v intervjuju zapisal: » Želimo si, da bi objekt zares zaživel. Stavba je dovolj prilagodljiva, da so v njej mogoči kakršnikoli programi. Predvsem mlajša populacija želi prostore, ki omogočajo demokratično vključevanje posameznikov. Upam, da jim bo Rog namenil vsaj del prostorov: da bodo našli svojo delavnico, kjer bodo lahko počeli vse, kar bi običajno počeli v garaži staršev. Želim si, da bi v Rogu našli dopolnilo tistemu, kar pogrešajo v vsakdanjem življenju. «[15]

»Kot ni bilo mogoče izbrisati ljudi, tudi ideje Avtonomne tovarne Rog ni mogoče ne izbrisati, ne uničiti. Prostori so uničeni, ideje in ljudje smo preživeli. Čeprav je bil uničen velik del arhiva izbrisanih, zgodovino tega boja, ki je tudi boj proti gentrifikaciji, predajamo prihajajočim generacijam.«[13]

Gentrifikacija predstavlja velik izziv nadaljnemu razvoju urbanih območij. Dokler se mesta razvijajo v smeri porabe in spektakla, lahko hitro podležejo gentrifikaciji in postanejo prostor za vedno bolj premožne uporabnike. Vskaršna prenova ali revitalizacija mestnega tkiva mora biti dobro premišljena, tako da gradi avtentičnost mesta in ne ruši obstoječe skupnosti. Če prenova tovarne Rog predstavlja kvaliteten primer prenove, pa sodelovanje investitorja z obstoječimi uporabniki predstavlja ravno nasprotno. Namesto participativnega oblikovanja je prišlo do nasilne evikcije ter izrinjenja. Kapital ubija? V primeru Roga je odgovor pritridilen. Kapital, ki za nekatere ubija, za druge gradi. Namesto da se sprašujemo, ali kapital ubija, bi se morali vprašati, ali kapital resnično prispeva k izgradnji skupnosti.
